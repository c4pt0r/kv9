The evidence therefore favors physical storage waits on the rotational data volume as the observed wall-time limiter. These conclusions are limited to the recorded interval, not every second of the audit.

## Prospective improvement, without weakening this acceptance

The most direct operational option for a future separately prepared campaign is capacity-qualified NVMe retention storage. The current root filesystem is NVMe while `/mnt/data` is rotational RAID5. Preserve all filesystem guards, complete hashes, frame/decoder checks and final readback; assess capacity first. No current files should be relocated and no current audit rerun is warranted.

A separately qualified reader could also combine **compressed-input hashing with feeding the decoder**, then retain the **independent full post-decode compressed rehash**. A bounded pump would read each compressed byte once, update a SHA/length accumulator, and supply exactly those bytes to the decoder; decoded output would still be completely hashed and counted. Final acceptance would require the consumed input hash/length to equal the original catalog, successful bounded decoding, the unchanged post-decode full content hash, and unchanged path/open-descriptor identities before and after. No restore, retirement, or success publication could occur before every check passes. All original corruption, replacement, truncation, trailing-frame, excess-output, nonzero-exit, timeout and child-cleanup rejection paths must remain, with bounded concurrent pipe draining to prevent deadlock.

This would reduce the source-level full compressed input work from three passes to two, **not guarantee a one-third elapsed-time reduction**: repeated passes may already hit cache. It changes when the first full compressed digest becomes known: during the decode rather than before decoder launch. If strict pre-launch full-hash validation itself must remain an invariant, this fusion is not equivalent and all three passes must be retained. A smaller compatible option is folding frame-extent parsing into the existing first hash pass while preserving the current pre-launch hash and post-decode rehash; that removes the separate seek walk but not a full content pass. No such implementation or qualification was performed here.

Future per-object timings for prehash, frame walk, decode and posthash would distinguish physical first-read cost from cached rechecks without inferring those costs from endpoint counters. They are a prospective measurement aid, not a new requirement on the successfully completed audit.
